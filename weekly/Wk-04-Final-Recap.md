# Week 4 Final Recap

Week 4 focused on synthesizing the previous three weeks of work into a single physical-AI capstone. Rather than introducing another independent model, the goal was to connect the Week 1 InGen/PIC 2.0 research, the Week 2 Aido Rover reinforcement-learning experiment, and the Week 3 Fari multimodal companion-AI pipeline into one coherent engineering narrative.

The main reinforcement-learning result remained the held-out evaluation of the Aido Rover PPO policy. After 200,000 training timesteps, PPO achieved **188.09 ± 35.26** mean episode reward across 20 held-out episodes, compared with **13.12 ± 50.45** for the rule-based baseline and **−84.89 ± 17.19** for the random baseline. The most important Week 4 interpretation was that physical-robot RL depends heavily on environment and reward validity. Unlike Breakout, where the game defines the score, the rover task required the desired operational behavior to be encoded into the reward function from first principles.

The multimodal track was summarized around the Fari distress-versus-normal classifier. The fused model combined 26 MFCC audio features, six visual facial-geometry features, and 384-dimensional MiniLM text embeddings. It achieved **precision, recall, F1, and AUROC of 1.000** on the held-out synthetic test set. Ablation showed that visual and text features were tied as the strongest individual modalities, while audio alone reached **F1 = 0.875** and **AUROC = 0.984**. Because the dataset was small and rule-generated, the final capstone emphasizes that these metrics demonstrate pipeline coherence rather than real-world elder-distress performance.

The cognitive-science and HCI synthesis centered on attentional capacity, theory of mind, and the difference between observable behavior and underlying mental state. These concepts led to concrete design recommendations for Fari: preserve uncertainty, use short and cognitively simple responses when possible distress is detected, avoid treating classifier outputs as diagnoses, and escalate only through explicit safety or human-supervision procedures.

The final cross-track conclusion is that physical AI should be viewed as an end-to-end pipeline: sensor observations are converted into perceptual features and state estimates, which support a decision policy, which must then pass through safety validation before producing an action. The multimodal Fari work explored the perception/state-understanding side of this loop, while the Aido Rover PPO work explored the decision-policy side. Week 4 connected those technical tracks into a single portfolio story centered on reproducible ML engineering, reward alignment, uncertainty, safety, and human-centered system design.
